# Scientific Background

## The Physics of Fusion

Nuclear fusion is the process by which atomic nuclei combine to form heavier nuclei, releasing energy.

**Key Fusion Reactions:**
- **Deuterium-Tritium (D-T):** Most promising for terrestrial fusion.
- **Deuterium-Deuterium (D-D):** Fuel availability but lower reactivity.
- **Proton-Boron (p-11B):** Aneutronic fusion, but challenging.

**Fusion Conditions:**
- **High temperature:** ~100 million degrees Celsius.
- **High pressure:** Confinement of plasma.
- **Sufficient confinement time:** Lawson criterion.

**Key Concepts:**
- **Plasma:** Ionized gas with charged particles.
- **Confinement:** Magnetic or inertial confinement.
- **Ignition:** Self-sustaining fusion reaction.
- **Breakeven:** Fusion energy equals input energy.

# Key Findings

## Finding 1: Magnetic Confinement Fusion — Tokamaks

Tokamaks are the most advanced magnetic confinement fusion devices.

**Key Tokamak Projects:**
- **ITER (International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor):** World's largest tokamak, under construction.
- **JET (Joint European Torus):** Leading fusion facility, setting records.
- **ASDEX Upgrade:** German tokamak contributing to ITER.
- **KSTAR:** Korean tokamak advancing plasma physics.

**Progress:**
- ITER commissioning and first plasma.
- Record plasma temperatures and confinement times.
- Advances in plasma control and diagnostics.

**Challenges:**
- Plasma instabilities and disruptions.
- Divertor heat loads and material erosion.
- Tritium breeding and fuel cycle.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications; international collaboration

---

## Finding 2: Magnetic Confinement Fusion — Stellarators

Stellarators offer an alternative approach to magnetic confinement.

**Key Stellarator Projects:**
- **Wendelstein 7-X (W7-X):** German stellarator, advanced design.
- **LHD (Large Helical Device):** Japanese stellarator for fusion research.

**Advantages:**
- No disruptions, steady-state operation.
- Advanced plasma control.

**Challenges:**
- Complex magnetic field design and construction.
- Higher plasma losses compared to tokamaks.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications

---

## Finding 3: Inertial Confinement Fusion

Inertial confinement fusion uses lasers or ion beams to compress and heat fuel.

**Key ICF Projects:**
- **NIF (National Ignition Facility):** US facility achieving record energy yields.
- **LMJ (Laser Mégajoule):** French facility for fusion research.
- **OMEGA:** US facility for plasma physics.

**Progress:**
- Record energy yields from laser fusion.
- Improved target design and physics understanding.
- Advances in diagnostics and simulation.

**Challenges:**
- Ignition and net energy gain.
- Target fabrication and injection.
- Laser efficiency and repetition rate.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications; government funding

---

## Finding 4: Private Fusion Companies

Private fusion companies are accelerating fusion development with venture capital funding.

**Key Companies:**
- **Commonwealth Fusion Systems (CFS):** High-temperature superconducting tokamaks.
- **TAE Technologies:** Advanced fusion concepts, proton-boron fusion.
- **General Fusion:** Magnetized target fusion.
- **Helion Energy:** Pulsed fusion, advanced concepts.

**Progress:**
- Prototype devices and proof-of-concept.
- Venture capital funding (billions invested).
- Collaboration with national labs and universities.

**Challenges:**
- Technical development and scaling.
- Funding and commercialization pathway.
- Regulatory and licensing frameworks.

**Evidence Status:** Industry reports; commercial announcements

---

## Finding 5: Fusion Fuel Cycle and Materials

Fuel cycle and materials are critical for fusion energy.

**Fuel Cycle:**
- **Tritium breeding:** Production of tritium from lithium.
- **Fuel recovery:** Separation and recycling of unburned fuel.
- **Waste management:** Low-level radioactive waste.

**Materials:**
- **Plasma-facing materials:** High heat flux, erosion resistance.
- **Structural materials:** Radiation resistance, thermal fatigue.
- **Superconducting materials:** High field magnets for confinement.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications; ongoing research
