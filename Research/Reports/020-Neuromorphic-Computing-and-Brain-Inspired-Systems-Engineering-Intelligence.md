# Scientific Background

## The Neuromorphic Computing Paradigm

Neuromorphic computing draws inspiration from biological neural systems to create energy-efficient and adaptive computing systems.

**Key Principles:**
- **Event-driven processing:** Computations triggered by input events.
- **Spiking neural networks (SNNs):** Models with temporal dynamics and spike-based communication.
- **Synaptic plasticity:** Learning through local, unsupervised rules.
- **Massive parallelism:** Many simple processing units working in parallel.

**Key Technologies:**
- **Spiking neuromorphic chips:** Hardware optimized for SNNs.
- **Memristors:** Resistive memory devices for synaptic weights.
- **Phase-change memory:** Synaptic plasticity in hardware.

# Key Findings

## Finding 1: Commercial Neuromorphic Systems

Neuromorphic systems are now commercially available for research and applications.

**Key Systems:**
- **Intel Loihi 2:** Research chip with synaptic plasticity and on-chip learning.
- **IBM TrueNorth:** Brain-inspired chip with 1 million neurons and 256 million synapses.
- **SpikeNN:** Low-power neural network accelerator for edge devices.
- **BrainChip Akida:** Commercial neuromorphic processor for edge AI.

**Applications:**
- **Edge computing:** Low-power AI for IoT devices.
- **Sensor processing:** Real-time processing of sensor data.
- **Autonomous systems:** Robotic control and navigation.
- **Pattern recognition:** Image, speech, and gesture recognition.

**Evidence Status:** Commercial announcements; technical publications

---

## Finding 2: Spiking Neural Networks

Spiking neural networks are advancing with novel learning algorithms and architectures.

**Key Developments:**
- **Surrogate gradient learning:** Enables backpropagation in spiking networks.
- **STDP (Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity):** Biologically-inspired learning rule.
- **Evolvable SNNs:** Evolutionary algorithms for network design.

**Applications:**
- **Event-based vision:** Processing of dynamic vision sensor data.
- **Robotics:** Real-time control and learning.
- **Pattern recognition:** Efficient classification of temporal patterns.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications; research prototypes

---

## Finding 3: Memristor-Based Systems

Memristors enable synaptic plasticity in neuromorphic hardware.

**Key Concepts:**
- **Memristors:** Resistive switching devices with memory of past states.
- **Synaptic arrays:** Crossbar arrays for matrix-vector multiplication.
- **On-chip learning:** Synaptic plasticity in hardware.

**Key Developments:**
- **RRAM (Resistive RAM):** Fast, non-volatile memory for synaptic weights.
- **Phase-change memory:** Synaptic plasticity with analog states.
- **Memristor crossbar arrays:** In-memory computing for neural networks.

**Applications:**
- **AI inference:** Energy-efficient neural network processing.
- **On-chip learning:** Real-time adaptation of synaptic weights.
- **Neuromorphic computing:** Hardware for brain-inspired systems.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications; industrial prototypes

---

## Finding 4: Integration with Conventional AI

Neuromorphic systems work alongside conventional AI for hybrid computing.

**Key Approaches:**
- **Heterogeneous systems:** Combine neuromorphic and conventional processors.
- **Event-driven acceleration:** Neuromorphic chips accelerate specific tasks.
- **Hybrid learning:** Combine unsupervised and supervised learning.

**Applications:**
- **Autonomous vehicles:** Neuromorphic for perception, conventional for decision-making.
- **Robotics:** Neuromorphic for control and learning.
- **Edge AI:** Neuromorphic for low-power processing.

**Evidence Status:** Research prototypes; industry demonstrations

---

## Finding 5: Large-Scale Brain-Inspired Architectures

Projects aim to simulate brain-scale neural networks.

**Key Projects:**
- **Human Brain Project:** Large-scale simulation of brain function.
- **SpiNNaker:** Spiking neural network architecture for simulation.
- **BrainScaleS:** Analog and digital neuromorphic systems.

**Challenges:**
- **Scale:** Simulating brain-scale networks requires extreme resources.
- **Connectivity:** Connecting billions of neurons and synapses.
- **Learning:** Enabling learning at scale.
- **Understanding:** Interpreting the behavior of large networks.

**Evidence Status:** Research publications; ongoing projects
