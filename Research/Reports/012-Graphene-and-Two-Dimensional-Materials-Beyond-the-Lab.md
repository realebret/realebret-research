# Abstract

Graphene and two-dimensional (2D) materials have matured from laboratory curiosities to commercially relevant materials with diverse applications. This report provides a comprehensive analysis of the field as of 2026, examining the production, characterization, and application of 2D materials beyond graphene, including transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs), MXenes, phosphorene, and others. Key developments include scalable synthesis methods, integration into commercial products, and emerging applications in electronics, energy, and medicine. The report explores how 2D materials are enabling new technologies and transforming existing industries.

# Scientific Background

## The 2D Materials Family

2D materials are crystalline materials consisting of a single layer of atoms or molecules.

**Key 2D Materials:**

| Material | Composition | Key Properties | Applications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graphene | Carbon | High conductivity, strength, flexibility | Electronics, composites, sensors |
| TMDs (MoS₂, WS₂) | Transition metal + chalcogen | Semiconducting, tunable bandgap | Electronics, optoelectronics, catalysis |
| MXenes | Transition metal carbide/nitride | Metallic conductivity, hydrophilic | Energy storage, EMI shielding, sensors |
| Phosphorene | Phosphorus | Tunable bandgap, high mobility | Electronics, photonics |
| Silicene | Silicon | Dirac fermions, compatibility with Si | Electronics |
| Borophene | Boron | Metallic, anisotropic | Electronics, catalysis |

# Key Findings

## Finding 1: Scalable Synthesis Methods

Scalable production methods enable industrial applications.

**Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD):**
- High-quality, large-area films for electronics.
- Roll-to-roll production for continuous manufacturing.
- Transfer methods for integration with substrates.

**Liquid-Phase Exfoliation:**
- Dispersion of 2D materials in solvents for inks and coatings.
- Scalable production for composites and energy applications.
- Cost-effective for large-volume applications.

**Electrochemical Exfoliation:**
- Fast, scalable production with tunable properties.
- Environmentally friendly processes.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications; industry adoption

## Finding 2: 2D Materials in Electronics

2D materials are enabling next-generation electronics.

**Transistors:**
- **MoS₂ transistors:** High mobility and on/off ratio for logic devices.
- **Flexible electronics:** 2D materials for bendable, stretchable devices.
- **Transparent electronics:** Graphene-based transparent conductors.

**Sensors:**
- **Gas sensors:** Highly sensitive detection of gases.
- **Biosensors:** Detection of biomolecules for diagnostics.
- **Strain sensors:** Wearable health monitoring.

**Optoelectronics:**
- **Photodetectors:** Broadband detection from UV to infrared.
- **LEDs and lasers:** Tunable emission wavelengths.
- **Solar cells:** 2D materials in photovoltaic devices.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications; commercial prototypes

## Finding 3: 2D Materials for Energy

2D materials are enabling energy storage and conversion.

**Batteries:**
- **MXene anodes:** High capacity and rate performance for Li-ion and Na-ion batteries.
- **Graphene electrodes:** Improved conductivity and cycling stability.
- **2D materials for solid-state batteries.**

**Supercapacitors:**
- **MXene electrodes:** High capacitance and rate capability.
- **Graphene composites:** Enhanced energy and power density.

**Catalysis:**
- **2D materials for electrocatalysis:** Hydrogen evolution, oxygen reduction, CO₂ reduction.
- **Photocatalysis:** Water splitting and pollutant degradation.

**Evidence Status:** Peer-reviewed publications; commercial development
